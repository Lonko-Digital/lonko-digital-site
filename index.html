Illustrative example · Synthetic data

This example demonstrates the Lonko approach using fictional information. It is not a customer result or a representation of a live account.

The question

Why are conversions declining even though advertising traffic remains relatively stable?

What did we find?

Traffic volume remained relatively stable during the comparison period, while the percentage of visitors completing the desired action declined.

What does it mean?

The available evidence suggests that traffic volume may not be the primary issue.

Something later in the customer journey—or a change in traffic quality—may be contributing to lower conversion performance.

That does not prove a landing-page, tracking, or campaign problem by itself.

What should you do next?

Review the areas most capable of explaining the change before increasing advertising spend.

Start with landing-page performance, conversion tracking, traffic quality, device differences, and recent campaign or website changes.

How confident are we?

Illustrative confidence: Moderate

The advertising evidence supports the direction of the finding, but additional website and analytics evidence would be required before identifying a definitive cause.

What's the evidence?

Traffic remained relatively stable while conversion efficiency declined during the same comparison period.

That relationship is enough to identify where investigation should begin—but not enough to claim a root cause.

Lonko doesn't manufacture certainty when the evidence isn't there. It shows what the evidence supports, what remains uncertain, and what deserves attention next.

Better decisions start with better questions.

Every meaningful Lonko analysis should help answer five questions.

  1. What did we find?

    Start with observable evidence rather than assumptions.

  2. What does it mean?

    Translate technical marketing signals into understandable business meaning.

  3. What should you do next?

    Turn the finding into a practical next step instead of leaving the user with another metric to interpret.

  4. How confident are we?

    Make the strength—and the limitations—of the conclusion visible.

  5. What's the evidence?

    Show what supports the recommendation so the reasoning can be understood, questioned, and validated.

The goal isn't to produce more information. It's to make the information you already have more useful.
